RDSP

A long-term savings plan for people with disabilities, with generous government support.

Discuss an RDSP

Main benefits

  • The government may add grants of up to 300% of what you contribute, based on family income
  • A bond of up to $1,000 a year may be paid in with no contribution at all for lower-income families
  • Growth inside the plan is not taxed while it stays there
  • The lifetime contribution limit is $200,000
  • Pairing the plan with life insurance can help provide for a loved one later

Who qualifies

The person must be a Canadian resident who qualifies for the Disability Tax Credit. The plan can be opened up to age 59, and grants and bonds are paid until the end of the year the person turns 49. The first step is usually confirming the Disability Tax Credit with the CRA.

Good to know

Withdrawals are partly taxable, and they are taxed in the hands of the person, often at a lower rate. A 10-year rule applies: if money is taken out within 10 years of receiving grants or bonds, those amounts must be repaid. If the plan holder passes away, the plan closes and the balance goes to the estate after any repayment.

Who it is for

  • People who qualify for the Disability Tax Credit
  • Parents and guardians planning for a child with a disability
  • Adults with a disability who want long-term savings
  • Families who want to claim the full grants and bonds available
  • Those who want an income that lasts alongside government benefits

How we help

  1. We map your goals. We look at what you want, when you want it, and what you already have.
  2. We build the plan. We choose the accounts and investments that fit your timeline and comfort with risk.
  3. We review it each year. We rebalance and update the plan when your income, family or goals change.

Common questions

Who can contribute?

The person, a parent, a family member or another approved contributor can add money, with the holder’s agreement. Contributions are not tax-deductible.

What are the grants and bonds?

The Canada Disability Savings Grant matches contributions, up to 300% depending on family income. The Canada Disability Savings Bond pays in up to $1,000 a year for lower-income families without any contribution.

Can I take money out early?

Yes, but if you withdraw within 10 years of receiving a grant or bond, those amounts must be repaid.

Does an RDSP affect other benefits?

Generally not. RDSP income is usually not counted against many provincial disability benefits, but the rules differ by province, so ask us before you open one.

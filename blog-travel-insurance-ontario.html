Do you need travel insurance when you leave Ontario?

Your provincial plan covers far less than most people think once you leave the country. Here is what travel insurance covers, and how to choose.

A medical emergency abroad can cost tens of thousands of dollars, or far more with a hospital stay or an air ambulance. Many Canadians assume their provincial health plan will cover it. In most cases it does not, or it pays only a small part of the bill. That gap is exactly what travel insurance is for.

What your provincial plan does and does not cover

OHIP and other provincial plans cover medically necessary care in Canada. Outside the country, they pay only limited amounts toward emergency care, and those amounts are usually a small fraction of what a hospital in the United States or elsewhere will charge. They do not cover things like medical evacuation, a return flight home, or the costs of a companion who stays with you. Rules also differ if you are outside your province but still in Canada, so check before you go.

What travel insurance covers

Type of coverageWhat it does
Emergency medicalPays for hospital, doctor, ambulance and prescription costs for a sudden illness or injury while away
Trip cancellationReimburses non-refundable costs if you have to cancel for a covered reason, such as illness before departure
Trip interruptionHelps with the cost of returning home early or rejoining your trip
Baggage and delayPays for lost, stolen or delayed baggage and extra costs from a major delay
Accidental death and dismembermentPays a benefit for serious accidental injury

Most people need at least emergency medical coverage. Trip cancellation and interruption matter when you have paid a lot in advance that you cannot get back.

Single trip, multi-trip or snowbird?

  • Single trip: covers one trip for specific dates. A good choice if you travel once a year.
  • Annual multi-trip: covers several trips of a set length within a year. Often cheaper than buying a separate policy each time if you travel often.
  • Extended stay or snowbird: for longer stays away from home. Provincial plans limit how long you can be out of province and keep coverage, so check your plan’s rules before a long trip.

Pre-existing conditions and stability

This is where most claim disputes begin. Insurers usually ask about your health on the application, and many exclude conditions that were not stable in the period before you left (the length of that period varies by policy). A change in medication or a new test result can affect whether a related claim is covered. Answer the health questions completely and honestly. If something changes after you buy the policy and before you leave, check whether you need to update it.

Is credit card coverage enough?

Some credit cards and group benefit plans include travel insurance, but the details matter. Common limitations include:

  • Age limits, with coverage reduced or ending past a certain age
  • A maximum number of days per trip
  • Lower coverage limits than you might expect
  • A requirement to charge the whole trip to the card
  • Strict rules about pre-existing conditions

Read the certificate of insurance, not just the marketing page, and compare it with a standalone policy. It is common to find that the card is a useful backup but not enough on its own.

When to buy

Buy trip cancellation coverage as soon as you book, because it often needs to be bought within a short window of your first deposit. Emergency medical can be bought just before you leave, but earlier is better since it also protects you if you become unwell before departure. Never wait until you are abroad.

Before you buy: a short checklist

  1. Confirm the coverage amount for emergency medical. Many policies offer at least $1 million, and a US trip may call for more.
  2. Check what counts as pre-existing and the stability period.
  3. Look at the deductible and any limits on specific costs.
  4. Check the policy covers your activities, such as skiing or scuba diving.
  5. Save the claims phone number and policy number somewhere easy to find while you travel.

Travel insurance is one of the easier things to arrange and one of the hardest to replace once something goes wrong. We can compare plans from several insurers and explain the differences in plain language.

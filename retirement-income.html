Retirement Income

Turn your savings into a steady income for life after work.

Your main options

  • RRIF (Registered Retirement Income Fund). Continues your RRSP and lets you take regular withdrawals. You must convert your RRSP by the end of the year you turn 71.
  • LIF (Life Income Fund). Does the same for money held in a LIRA.
  • Annuities. Turn part of your savings into a guaranteed income.

Start early

If you are within about 15 years of retirement, review your plan now. We estimate your income needs and match them to the options above.

Other income sources to plan around

Most retirees combine several sources: the Canada Pension Plan (CPP), Old Age Security (OAS), workplace pensions and personal savings. The timing of each matters, and we help you choose the order and amounts that last.

Who it is for

  • People within 10 to 15 years of retirement
  • Recent retirees turning RRSPs into income
  • Those with a LIRA or workplace pension
  • Couples planning to share income
  • Anyone worried about outliving their savings

How we help

  1. We map your goals. We look at what you want, when you want it, and what you already have.
  2. We build the plan. We choose the accounts and investments that fit your timeline and comfort with risk.
  3. We review it each year. We rebalance and update the plan when your income, family or goals change.

Common questions

When should I take CPP?

You can start as early as 60 or as late as 70. Starting later raises the monthly amount. The best age depends on your health, other income and plans.

How much can I withdraw from a RRIF?

There is a minimum each year based on your age. Withdrawals are taxed as income. A LIF has both a minimum and a maximum.

How do I make my money last?

We estimate your spending, test different withdrawal rates, and plan for taxes and inflation.

What is an annuity?

An annuity turns a lump sum into a guaranteed income for life or a set period. It gives certainty but less flexibility.

Term or permanent life insurance?

Two types of life insurance do different jobs. Here is how to tell which one fits your situation, and when it makes sense to have both.

InsuranceSeptember 29, 20266 min readBy Shreem Financial Group

In this article

  2. Permanent life insurance
  3. Term vs permanent at a glance
  4. How much do you need?
  5. Can you have both?
  6. Common mistakes

Life insurance pays a tax-free amount to the people you name if you pass away. What confuses most people is that there are two very different kinds. Term insurance is simple protection for a set period. Permanent insurance covers you for life and can build a cash value. Neither is better in general. The right one depends on what you are trying to protect and for how long.

Term life insurance

Term insurance covers you for a fixed period, commonly 10, 20 or 30 years, or until a certain age. If you pass away during the term, your beneficiaries receive the death benefit. If you outlive the term, the policy ends and nothing is paid out, much like car insurance when you never claim.

  • Lowest cost for the amount of coverage, especially when you are young and healthy
  • Premiums are usually level for the whole term, then rise sharply at renewal
  • Often convertible: many policies let you switch to permanent insurance later without a new medical exam, if you do so before the conversion deadline

Term is a good match for needs that go away over time: a mortgage, young children who will become independent, income your family relies on until you retire, or a business loan.

Permanent life insurance

Permanent insurance is designed to last your whole life as long as the premiums are paid. It generally costs more because the insurer will pay out eventually, and part of what you pay builds a cash value inside the policy. There are two main types:

  • Whole life has fixed premiums, a guaranteed death benefit and a guaranteed cash value. Participating policies may also earn dividends.
  • Universal life is more flexible, letting you adjust premiums and choose how the cash value is invested, with the trade-off that you take on more of the risk and have to monitor it.

Permanent coverage tends to suit needs that never go away: leaving an inheritance, covering the tax bill on an estate, providing for a child or dependant with lifelong needs, or business planning such as a buy-sell agreement.

How much do you need?

A common rule of thumb is ten times your annual income, but a better approach is to add up what your family would actually need. Think about:

  • Your mortgage and other debts
  • Income replacement for the years your family would need it
  • Education costs for your children
  • Final expenses and taxes
  • Subtracting any savings, group insurance through work and other resources you already have

Group coverage through an employer is useful, but it is usually tied to the job and may end if you leave. It is a reason to look at an individual policy, not a replacement for one.

Can you have both?

Yes, and many families do. A common approach is to use term insurance for the large, temporary needs (the mortgage and the years the children depend on you) and a smaller permanent policy for what lasts: final expenses, an inheritance or estate taxes. A layered plan like this usually costs less than covering everything with permanent insurance.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting. Premiums rise with age, and a health issue later can make coverage costlier or unavailable.
  • Relying only on coverage tied to a loan or a job, which may shrink or end when circumstances change.
  • Forgetting the beneficiary. Review who is named whenever your life changes, because the designation overrides your will.
  • Buying permanent insurance mainly as an investment without comparing it with other ways of saving.
  • Not answering the application honestly. A misstatement can lead to a claim being reduced or denied.

An advisor can compare policies from several insurers, explain the differences and help you decide how much coverage you need. Ask for quotes on both term and permanent so you can see the real cost difference for your age and health.

Term or permanent life insurance?

Two types of life insurance do different jobs. Here is how to tell which one fits.

Term insurance covers you for a set number of years, often the length of a mortgage or the years your children depend on you. It usually costs less for the same amount of cover. Permanent insurance is designed to last your whole life and can support estate and legacy planning. Many families use term to protect big debts now and add permanent cover later. An advisor can compare both from several insurers before you decide.

RRSP or TFSA: where should your next dollar go?

Both are registered accounts, but they save tax at different times.

An RRSP gives you a deduction when you contribute and you pay tax when you withdraw. A TFSA gives no deduction, but growth and withdrawals are generally tax-free. The right mix depends on your income now, your expected income in retirement and what you are saving for. Many people use both. Ask your advisor to look at your contribution room and goals before choosing.

Five questions to ask before you renew your mortgage

Your lender's renewal letter is an offer, not the only option.

Before you sign, ask what rate and term you are being offered, whether you can switch lenders without penalty, how prepayment options work, what happens if you sell during the term, and how the new payment fits your budget. Comparing at least two other options can show whether the renewal offer is competitive.

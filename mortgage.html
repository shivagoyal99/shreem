Buying a home

We work out what you can afford and what the monthly payment looks like, then compare lenders for the best fit.

  • Pre-approval. Shows your budget and holds a rate for a set period, often 90 to 120 days, so you can shop with confidence.
  • First-time buyers. Ask about the FHSA, the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan and possible land transfer tax rebates.
  • Down payment. Putting down less than 20% generally means mortgage default insurance, which is added to your mortgage.
  • Costs beyond the down payment. Legal fees, land transfer tax, inspection and moving costs. We help you plan for them.

Buying an investment property

Whether it is a rental condo or a multi-unit building, we look at the property and your overall finances together.

  • Down payment. A property you will not live in generally needs a larger down payment than one you will occupy.
  • Rental income. Some lenders count part of the expected rent when you qualify. How much depends on the lender.
  • Cash flow. We build in mortgage payments, property tax, insurance, maintenance and possible vacancy.
  • Tax. Talk to your accountant about how rental income and expenses are treated.

Refinancing and renewal

Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one. Renewal is the chance to review your terms when your term ends.

  • Use your equity. Borrow against the value you have built for renovations, a purchase or other plans.
  • Debt consolidation. Roll higher-interest debt into your mortgage. We show the full cost so you can see whether it saves money.
  • Renewal. The first offer is not always the best. We compare it with the market, and you can often switch lenders at renewal. See our five questions to ask before you renew.
  • Penalties. We show any penalty on your current mortgage before you decide.

Commercial mortgages

Financing for offices, retail space, industrial buildings and multi-unit properties, for owner-operators and investors.

  • How lenders look at it. They consider the property, its income and your business or personal finances.
  • Terms differ. Commercial mortgages often have shorter terms and amortizations and larger down payments than home mortgages.
  • Documents. Expect to provide financial statements, tax returns and details of the property or lease.
  • Timeline. Appraisals and reviews can take longer, so it helps to start early.
Talk about commercial financing

Fixed or variable?

  • Fixed rate: your rate and payment stay the same for the term, which makes budgeting simple.
  • Variable rate: the rate moves with the lender's prime rate, so your payment or the share going to interest can change.
  • The right choice depends on how much change in payments you can handle and how long you plan to keep the mortgage.

What you will need

  • Government photo ID
  • Proof of income, such as pay stubs, a letter of employment or your last two years of Notices of Assessment if you are self-employed
  • Proof of your down payment
  • Details of the property and, for a renewal, your current mortgage statement

Costs to plan for besides the down payment

  • Legal fees and title insurance
  • Ontario land transfer tax
  • Appraisal or inspection costs
  • Home insurance, property tax adjustments and moving costs

How we help

  1. We talk. Your income, savings, goals and timeline.
  2. We compare. Rates, terms and features from different lenders, with the full cost of each shown.
  3. We apply and follow through. We help gather documents, submit the application and stay with you until closing or renewal.

Common questions

How much down payment do I need?

The minimum depends on the purchase price. Putting down less than 20% generally means paying for mortgage default insurance, which is added to your mortgage.

What is the mortgage stress test?

Lenders check that you could still afford the payments at a higher qualifying rate than the one you will actually pay. It is built into pre-approval, so we plan around it.

What happens if I leave my mortgage before the term ends?

You may owe a prepayment penalty, and the amount depends on your lender and rate type. We show the formula before you sign.

Can I switch lenders at renewal?

Often yes, usually without a prepayment penalty, though there may be legal or admin costs. Check your own mortgage terms.

Can I take my mortgage with me if I move?

Many mortgages are portable, which lets you move the rate and balance to a new home within certain limits. Ask us before you sign.

General information only, not personal, financial or legal advice. Rates, rules and eligibility change and depend on the lender and your situation. Speak with an advisor, and a lawyer where relevant, before you decide.

Investment Funds

Segregated funds, non-registered accounts and GICs, and how they differ.

Segregated funds

Like mutual funds, but sold by insurance companies with guarantees on part of your investment at maturity and on death. They can name a beneficiary and may offer creditor protection, depending on the province. They can be held in an RRSP, TFSA, LIRA, LIF or non-registered plan.

Non-registered accounts

Flexible accounts with no contribution limit. Tax is paid on interest, dividends and gains outside the plan. Many people use them once their RRSP and TFSA are full.

GICs

Guaranteed Investment Certificates pay a set rate for a set term. They suit money you need on a known date.

Who it is for

  • Investors who want professional management
  • People who want a guarantee on part of their investment
  • Those who want to name a beneficiary and avoid probate
  • Business owners seeking creditor protection options
  • Savers who want a simple, diversified approach

How we help

  1. We map your goals. We look at what you want, when you want it, and what you already have.
  2. We build the plan. We choose the accounts and investments that fit your timeline and comfort with risk.
  3. We review it each year. We rebalance and update the plan when your income, family or goals change.

Common questions

What is the difference between a segregated fund and a mutual fund?

A segregated fund is an insurance contract that invests in a fund and can offer guarantees and a named beneficiary. It usually costs more than a similar mutual fund.

Are GICs safe?

GICs from eligible institutions are generally covered by deposit insurance up to set limits. Your return is fixed, so it may not keep pace with inflation.

Can I lose money?

Investment values can go down. Guarantees on segregated funds limit the loss at maturity or death, subject to the contract's terms.

What do the funds cost?

Funds charge a management fee, and segregated funds may charge more for the guarantees. We explain the costs before you decide.
